HOME VARIABLES & CHILD ABUSE

EMPIRICAL STUDIES IN RIVERS STATE, NIGERIA
DR. CHINEDU MERCEDES CHIDI-UGBAJA
PAPER I (IJIPSD PDF) PAPER II (IJISSHR PDF) ZENODO ARCHIVE VIEW ABSTRACTS CALL: +234 7039593580 WHATSAPP CHAT
"Child sexual abuse constitutes an acute disruption of adolescent mental health, personal bodily autonomy, and academic development. Identifying predictive home variables is the first crucial step toward institutional protection and evidence-based guidance counseling."

In a groundbreaking empirical study conducted across the educational landscape of Rivers State, Nigeria, Dr. Chinedu Mercedes Chidi-Ugbaja (Ph.D.) investigates the domestic predictors and behavioral dynamics that influence adolescent vulnerability to sexual abuse. Grounded in the Department of Educational Psychology, Guidance and Counselling at Rivers State University, Port Harcourt, this work bridges rigorous psychological inquiry with urgent community protection strategies.

Drawing from a target population of 197,428 public senior secondary school students, the study utilized the Taro Yamane scientific sampling formula to evaluate 399 male and female students across nine secondary institutions representative of all three senatorial districts in Rivers State.

Published across two companion peer-reviewed volumes in the International Journal of Innovative Psychology & Social Development (IJIPSD) and the International Journal of Innovative Social Sciences & Humanities Research (IJISSHR), Dr. Chidi-Ugbaja’s research delivers high-impact statistical modeling and actionable guidance frameworks for educators, counselors, parents, and policymakers.

CORE RESEARCH FINDINGS

Rigorous statistical evaluations derived from multi-stage sampling across Rivers State senatorial districts.

52.1%

Family Conflict Dynamics

Data proves that marital discord, domestic disputes, and chronic conflict within the home create emotional alienation, significantly escalating child vulnerability to predatory approaches.

59.8%

Family Structure Disruption

Non-intact household structures, irregular custody situations, and single-parent stressors were statistically identified as major predictor variables influencing adolescent supervision gaps.

67.6%

Non-Biological Caregivers

The presence of non-biological guardians, co-habitants, and domestic domestic workers was evaluated by over two-thirds of respondents as a paramount predictor requiring structured protective oversight.

ACADEMIC PROFILE

DR. CHINEDU MERCEDES CHIDI-UGBAJA, Ph.D.

Dr. Chinedu Mercedes Chidi-Ugbaja is an optometrist, professional counsellor, and evangelist with three decades of clinical practice. Graduating in October 1996 from the College of Optometry, Faculty of Medical and Health Sciences, she has long directed a private eye care clinic in Port Harcourt, Rivers State. Recognizing the vital connection between physical health and emotional well-being, she expanded her expertise into Guidance and Counselling, earning a diploma, a master’s degree, and ultimately her doctorate in 2026.

Driven by a calling of GOD to serve, Dr. Chinedu is the visionary pioneer of the Intercessorise Outreach in her hometown of Arondizuogu, Imo State. Through this initiative, she coordinates an annual outreach providing free eye care, screenings, and treatments to underserved communities. Her work uniquely bridges her clinical background as an optometrist, her professional insight as a counsellor, and her dedication as an evangelist to deliver holistic care.

Outside her professional practice and ministry, Dr. Chinedu is a devoted wife and a mother of five children. Her ministry, The Intercessorise Outreach, also maintains a liaison office at the popular Ama-Edward to remain closely connected and accessible to the communities she serves.
